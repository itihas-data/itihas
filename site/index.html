A real-time database of Indian state GDP

State GDP figures in India are published early and then revised for about three years. ITIHAS keeps every published version, with its date and its source document, so that anyone can see what was known about a state’s economy at any point in time.

What is in the database

The database brings together three kinds of source: the state governments’ own documents, releases from the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, and the Reserve Bank of India’s Handbook of Statistics on Indian States. Every figure is stored with the date it first became public.

02

The two-year rule

An early estimate of a state’s growth says little about where the figure will finally settle. For about two years after a fiscal year ends, estimates agree only loosely with the latest figure. After 24 months they agree closely. We call this the two-year rule.

03

The league table

Rankings of states by growth are often quoted as soon as the first figures appear. Those early rankings change a great deal. Many states move several places once the figures are revised, and only two to four of the first top five stay there.

04

Where the money would have gone

State GDP is one input to the formula that divides central taxes among the states. We recomputed the 15th Finance Commission’s shares with later, revised figures. Over 2021 to 2026, ₹25,654 crore would have gone to different states. This is a question of when data become reliable, not of whether the formula is fair.
